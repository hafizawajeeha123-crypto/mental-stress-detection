Research Paper

Abstract

Mental health disorders represent one of the most pressing global health challenges, affecting over 1 billion people worldwide. Traditional diagnostic pathways reliant on in-person clinical assessment are costly, slow, and inaccessible to large populations, particularly in low-resource settings. Social media platforms, where individuals frequently express genuine emotional states, offer an unprecedented corpus of real-world psychological signal.


This paper presents MindSense AI, a comparative machine learning study for automated mental stress detection from short form text. We evaluate six classical ML classifiers Logistic Regression, Linear SVM, K-Nearest Neighbours, Decision Tree, Random Forest, and Naïve Bayes against a curated dataset of 212,362 labelled social media statements spanning seven mental health categories: Normal, Depression, Suicidal, Anxiety, Stress, Bipolar, and Personality Disorder.

Related Work

Literature Review

Coppersmith et al., 2025
Quantifying Mental Health Signals in Twitter
Pioneered the use of Twitter data for detecting PTSD, depression, bipolar disorder, and seasonal affective disorder through linguistic analysis of user timelines.
✦ Finding: Language use significantly differs across mental health conditions on social media.
Resnik et al., 2022
Beyond LDA: Exploring Supervised Topic Modelling
Applied supervised LDA topic models to Reddit posts from mental health subreddits, showing that topic-based features improve over pure bag-of-words baselines for depression detection.
✦ Finding: Semantic topic features outperform raw TF-IDF for nuanced mental health detection.
Gkotsis et al., 2024
Characterisation of Mental Health Conditions in Social Media
Used 14 mental health subreddits to build multiclass classifiers with SVM and LR, demonstrating the viability of Reddit as a clinical NLP corpus. Achieved 71–78% accuracy on multi-class tasks.
✦ Finding: Linear SVM consistently competitive across mental health text classification tasks.
Linthicum et al., 2023
Machine Learning in Suicide Risk Research
Systematic review of 40+ ML studies for suicide risk prediction. Found that ensemble models (Random Forest, Gradient Boosting) consistently outperformed single classifiers, with text features being highly informative.
✦ Finding: Ensemble methods are gold standard for suicide risk classification — confirmed in our results.

Dataset Overview

Mental Stress Detection dataset

The dataset used in this project contains real-world textual statements collected from social media platforms and online mental health discussions. Each text statement is labeled with a specific mental health category such as Anxiety, Depression, Stress, Bipolar, Suicidal, or Normal. The dataset is designed for multi-class text classification and is used to train Machine Learning models for detecting mental health conditions from user-generated text.

Research Summary

Conclusion

Key findings, contributions, and the road ahead for mental health NLP.

🏆
Key Findings
Random Forest with TF-IDF bigram features achieves 91.9% accuracy — the best among six evaluated classifiers — on a balanced 7-class mental health dataset of 212K samples. Ensemble methods consistently outperform single-model approaches. Class-wise F1 scores range from 0.86 (Personality Disorder) to 0.94 (Normal), indicating strong generalisation across all categories.
🔬
Research Contributions
  • First systematic 6-model comparison on this 7-class mental health corpus
  • Demonstrated TF-IDF bigram effectiveness for psychiatric text classification
  • Identified critical confusion between Depression and Suicidal classes
  • Produced a deployable, browser-embedded real-time classifier
  • Quantified performance ceiling of classical ML in healthcare NLP
⚠️
Limitations
TF-IDF cannot model word order, negation, or sarcasm. The Depression–Suicidal boundary remains the hardest classification boundary (562 cross-class misclassifications), posing clinical risk. The model was trained on English Reddit/forum text and may not generalise to other languages, platforms, or clinical settings without retraining.
🚀
Future Work
  • Fine-tune MentalBERT / RoBERTa for 96–99% target accuracy
  • Add temporal modelling (post sequences, not single posts)
  • Extend to multilingual support (Urdu, Hinglish)
  • Integrate explainability via SHAP or LIME
  • Prospective clinical validation with licensed psychiatrists
  • Build a real-time API with therapist alert system
